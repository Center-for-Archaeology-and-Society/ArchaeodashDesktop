## **Two Modes of Operation**

ArchaeoDash runs in two modes with different storage and account characteristics:

- **Desktop mode** runs entirely on your computer. Your projects, group files, transformations, and results are files in directories you control. No account is created and no project data is transmitted to us.
- **Hosted mode** runs on our servers. You sign in with an account; your projects and their files are stored in the hosted file store under your account, and your account, session, security, and preference records are stored in the service database.

## **User Accounts**

### **Registration**

To use hosted features, you must register for an account with a username and email address, agree to this notice, and verify your email address before your first login. If you forget your password, use the *Forgot your password?* option in the login dialog to request a reset email. Reset links are single-use and expire within 24 hours.

Passwords are never stored in readable form. If you choose "remember me", your session persists for the duration you select (30 or 90 days); you can log out on one device or everywhere at any time.

## **Your Data, Ownership, and Backups**

You retain all rights to your project data. In desktop mode your data never leaves your machine. In hosted mode:

- Your project, group, and source files are stored as self-describing files in the hosted user file store; the account database holds only identity, security, and preference records — never your analytical data.
- Transformations you save are explicit, versioned project artifacts you can inspect, export, or delete.
- Analysis results (PCA, UMAP, LDA, clustering, distances) are ephemeral unless you explicitly export or save them.
- Group files are self-describing and remain complete on their own; edits are tracked as revisions so files stay readable even outside the application.
- **Back up your own data regularly.** The hosted service keeps operational backups for disaster recovery only (about 30 days' retention); this is not a substitute for your own backups, and no guarantee is made regarding persistence of data storage.
